Summar y
This paper introduces OrganoidEnv, a Gymnasium-compatible environment
that couples an external deep reinforcement learning (D3QN) controller to a
500-neuron, metabolically constrained Izhikevich spiking neural network (SNN)
via current-based sensory–motor stimulation. The SNN incorporates a
dopamine-modulated dual-trace eligibility rule, a Global Activity Regulator
(GAR), inhibitory homeostasis, and structural pruning to maintain stability and
learn from delayed rewards. On a four-stage 2D navigation curriculum, the
system reports 70.8% overall success (single run), with ablations suggesting
the necessity of the stabilizers and dual-trace plasticity.

intermittently overwriting internal SNN states. A more principled

Overall Assessment
The paper addresses an important and timely problem—closing the loop
between biologically constrained SNNs and external RL agents under realistic
stability and plasticity constraints—and offers an appealing conceptual bridge
to organoid intelligence. The systems integration (metabolic constraints, dual-
trace plasticity, GAR, inhibitory scaling, pruning) into an open-source
environment is promising, and the ablation trends are intuitively consistent
with the proposed mechanisms. However, the current manuscript has
substantial shortcomings that preclude publication in its present form: key
architectural ambiguities (particularly the sensory-to-SNN pathway), missing
methodological details needed for replication, single-seed evaluations without
variance estimates, and several contradictory or placeholder figures that
undermine confidence in the reported results. Baseline choices are also
misaligned with the proposed RL backbone, and important comparisons to
contemporary SNN learning methods are absent.
I recommend major revisions. Resolving the architectural inconsistencies, fully
specifying parameters and stimulation protocols, correcting the figures, adding
fair and comprehensive baselines (including D3QN-only and SNN training
alternatives), and providing multi-seed/sensitivity analyses would substantially
strengthen the technical case and the contribution’s value to the Frontiers in
Neuroscience community.
